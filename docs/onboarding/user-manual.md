# Amore Cost user manual

*Alamin kung may kwenta ang presyo mo.* Know whether your price is
actually earning for you.

This guide is for food business owners: home bakers, carinderias, food
carts, online food sellers. You don't need any technical background.
Every example uses real numbers from the app's sample recipes, so you
can follow along on your own screen.

## 1. What Amore Cost does

Most small food businesses set prices by guessing, or by copying the
stall next door. Amore Cost replaces the guess with three answers:

1. **What does one serving really cost me?** It counts every
   ingredient, including the parts you throw away, plus packaging.
2. **What should I charge?** It suggests a price based on the profit you
   want, with VAT and Senior/PWD discounts worked out correctly.
3. **Does that price pay my rent and bills?** It spreads your monthly
   expenses across your dishes and shows whether you break even.

It's a **pricing tool**, not a cash register. It doesn't record real
sales or print official receipts. Use it to decide your prices, then put
those prices in whatever POS or menu board you already use.

## 2. Before you start

**No sign-up.** Open the app and start. No email, no password.

**Your data stays on this device only.** Everything you type is saved
in this browser on this phone or computer. That keeps it private, but:
- If you clear your browser data, or switch to a new phone or computer,
  your recipes **won't** come with you.
- **Back up regularly.** On the **Dashboard**, tap **Download full
  backup**. This saves everything as one file. Email it to yourself or
  put it in Google Drive. To bring it back, tap **Restore from backup**
  on the same card.

**It's free.** Nothing is locked.

**English or Tagalog.** Tap **EN / TL** at the top of any page to
switch. Next to it, **Light / Dark / Auto** changes the screen colors.

**On your phone,** tap the **☰** button at the top left to open the
menu.

## 3. Your first 2 minutes: try the sample recipes

The fastest way to learn the app is to look at a filled-in example.

1. Open the **Dashboard** (or **Input**). While you have no recipes, you'll see
   **Try with sample recipes**. Tap it.
2. The app loads three Filipino dishes:
   - **Chicken Adobo with rice**
   - **Tapsilog**
   - **Halo-Halo (to go)**

   It also loads 13 ingredients and a sample set of monthly bills for a
   small carinderia: stall rent, electricity, LPG, two kitchen staff and
   Facebook ads, totaling ₱52,700/month.
3. A blue bar says **"You're looking at sample recipes."** Explore every
   page. Change numbers and see what happens. Nothing you do here can
   break anything.
4. When you're ready for your own data, tap **Remove sample recipes**.
   Only the samples are removed. Any recipe you added yourself stays.

> The sample bills are only added if your OPEX page is still empty.
> If you've already entered your own expenses, they are never replaced.

## 5. Dashboard

The Dashboard is your home page.

- **Four number cards:**
  - **Active recipes:** how many dishes you've costed.
  - **Monthly OPEX:** your monthly bills, including any safety buffer.
  - **Avg food cost %:** on average, how much of your menu price goes
    to ingredients. The healthy range is **28–32%**. It turns
    **red** when it's too high.
  - **Ingredients in library:** how many ingredients you've saved.
- **Needs your attention:** lists any dish earning less than the target
  margin you set for it, so you know which prices to fix first.
- **Recent recipes:** your latest dishes with cost, price and food cost
  %. Tap **Edit** to open one.
- **Dish overhead allocation:** type how many dishes you sell a month
  and see how much rent and bills each plate must cover. This is the
  same number as on the OPEX page.
- **Back up your data:** **Download full backup** and **Restore from
  backup**.

## 6. Input: your recipes

This is where you'll spend most of your time.

### 6.1 Add a recipe

1. Tap **+ New recipe**, type a name (for example, *Pancit Canton*), and
   tap **Add recipe**.
2. The **Edit & Breakdown** window opens right away. Everything you
   type **saves automatically**. There's no Save button. Close the
   window when you're done.

### 6.2 The three cards at the top

As you type ingredients, these update instantly:

| Card | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recipe cost** (orange) | What one serving costs you to make |
| **Suggested price** (green) | What to charge the customer, VAT included if you turned VAT on |
| **Profit per serving** (blue) | What you keep from each serving after the recipe cost, before rent and bills |

The green card also shows the **Senior/PWD price**: what a Senior
Citizen or PWD pays for this dish by law.

### 6.3 Add ingredients

Each row is one ingredient. Fill in:
- **Portion qty** and **Portion unit:** how much **one serving** uses
  (for example, 200 g).
- **Unit cost:** what that ingredient costs per unit.

**Example: Chicken Adobo with rice (one serving)**

| Ingredient | Portion | Bought at | Yield % | Real cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken (cut-up) | 200 g | ₱220/kg | 70% | ₱62.86 |
| Soy sauce | 30 mL | ₱90/L | — | ₱2.70 |
| Cane vinegar | 30 mL | ₱60/L | — | ₱1.80 |
| Garlic | 15 g | ₱160/kg | 85% | ₱2.82 |
| Cooking oil | 10 mL | ₱120/L | — | ₱1.20 |
| Rice | 150 g | ₱58/kg | — | ₱8.70 |
| **Recipe cost** | | | | **₱80.08** |

You can enter the recipe in **grams** even when you buy in **kilos**.
The app converts automatically, and a small green note appears under
the ingredient name, such as "Converted to kg at ₱220/kg."

### 6.4 Yield %: count what you throw away

You pay for the whole chicken, bones included. If 1 kg of bone-in
chicken gives only 700 g of meat, your **yield is 70%**.

Type **70** in the **Yield %** column, and the app raises the cost to
cover the waste:

> 200 g of chicken at ₱220/kg = ₱44.00 bought
> ₱44.00 ÷ 70% = **₱62.86 real cost**

Without yield, this adobo would look ₱19 cheaper per plate than it
really is. Your price would be too low and you'd lose money on every
plate. Leave Yield % blank for ingredients with no waste (oil, soy
sauce, rice).

**Typical yields to start with:**

| Ingredient | Yield |
|---|---|
| Bone-in chicken | 65–75% |
| Whole fish | 40–50% |
| Pork with skin and bones | 70–80% |
| Garlic and onions | 85–90% |
| Leafy vegetables | 75–85% |

These are rough starting points. Weigh your own before and after
prepping once, and use your real number.

### 6.5 Buying in packs or sacks

If you buy flour by the sack, use the **package columns** instead of
working out the unit cost yourself:

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| **Supplier** | SM Market |
| **Package price** | ₱1,200 |
| **Qty per pack** | 25 |
| **Pack unit** | kg |
| **Shipping fee** | ₱50 |
| **Portion qty / unit** | 250 g |

The app works out: (₱1,200 + ₱50) ÷ 25 kg = ₱50/kg = **₱0.05/g**, so
250 g costs **₱12.50**.

### 6.6 Packaging, labor and overhead

Below the ingredients:
- **Packaging:** the container, cup, lid, bag or utensils for one
  serving. For example, the halo-halo sample has ₱8 for a to-go cup.
- **Labor:** hours × rate per hour, if you want a specific dish's prep
  time counted. If you already pay staff as a monthly salary on the
  OPEX page, **leave labor at zero here** so you don't count wages
  twice.
- **Overhead allocation:** your share of rent and bills per serving.
  Tap **Estimate from OPEX** to fill it from your OPEX page.

### 6.7 How the price is worked out

Open **Advanced Pricing Settings** to choose how the suggested price is
calculated.

| Method | What it means | Recipe cost ₱100 → price |
|---|---|---|
| **Margin %** (default) | Profit as a share of the **selling price** | 40% margin → **₱166.67** |
| **Markup %** | Profit as a share of the **cost** | 40% markup → **₱140.00** |
| **Fixed profit** | A peso amount on top of cost | ₱50 profit → **₱150.00** |

Margin and markup are **not** the same number. 40% margin earns much
more than 40% markup. For food, a margin of **65–72%** gives a food
cost of about **28–35%**.

Here you also choose **tax**: No tax, VAT (12%), Percentage Tax (3%),
or a custom rate. If you're VAT-registered, choose VAT and the
suggested price will include it.

**Servings:** if one batch makes many servings (a tray of 24 brownies),
enter **24**. The app shows total cost, revenue and profit for the
whole batch.

### 6.8 Round the menu price

Nobody puts ₱298.97 on a menu board. Use the dropdown on the green
**Suggested price** card:

| Option | Adobo (₱298.97) | Halo-Halo (₱180.69) |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact** | ₱298.97 | ₱180.69 |
| **Round up ₱1** | ₱299 | ₱181 |
| **Round up ₱5** | ₱300 | ₱185 |
| **Round up ₱10** | ₱300 | ₱190 |
| **Ends in 9** | ₱299 | ₱189 |

- The app **always rounds up**, never down, so you never quietly earn
  less than the margin you chose.
- The card shows "rounded up from ₱298.97" so you can see the
  difference.
- **Profit per serving** and food cost % update to match the rounded
  price.

### 6.9 Target margin

At the top of the editor, **Target margin** is the lowest margin you
accept for this dish (30% by default). If the dish earns less, it shows
as **Below target** on the Input page and in the Dashboard's "Needs your
attention" list.

### 6.10 Working with your recipe list

- **Search** by name.
- **All recipes / On target / Below target:** filter which dishes you
  see.
- **Show ingredient breakdown:** a quick look without opening the
  editor.
- **Edit breakdown:** opens the full editor.
- **Duplicate:** copy a recipe to make a variation (for example,
  *Adobo, large serving*).
- **Delete:** removes it after you confirm.

### 6.11 Ingredient Library: change a price once, update every recipe

The **Ingredient Library** button at the top of Input is your shared
price list.

1. Tap **Ingredient Library** and use **Add an ingredient**. Enter a
   name, unit, **Cost / unit**, and optionally a category (for example,
   "meat").
2. In a recipe, pick that ingredient from the small dropdown above the
   ingredient name. The row is now **linked**.
3. When the price changes at the market, update it **once** in the
   library, and every recipe that uses it recalculates.

**Example:** chicken goes from ₱220/kg to ₱300/kg. Change it once in the
library. The adobo's chicken line goes from ₱62.86 to ₱85.71, and its
recipe cost and suggested price update by themselves.

You can also log several **suppliers** for each ingredient. The library
marks the **cheapest**, and **Use this price** applies it. Ingredients
with the same category appear as **Similar ingredients**, which helps
when you're looking for a cheaper substitute.

## 7. OPEX & Overhead: your monthly bills

**OPEX** means the bills you pay every month whether you sell one plate
or a thousand: rent, kuryente, tubig, LPG, sahod, ads.

1. Tap **Add expense** for each bill. Give it a name, a monthly
   amount, and a **Category** (Rent, Utilities, Labor, Marketing,
   Software, Other).
2. The **Category breakdown** shows where your money goes.
3. **Contingency buffer:** an extra % for surprises like a broken
   freezer or a price hike. **10%** is a common start.
4. **Estimated dishes sold per month:** your best guess of plates sold
   across your whole menu.

**Example (the sample carinderia):**

| Expense | Category | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Stall rent | Rent | ₱15,000 |
| Electricity & water | Utilities | ₱6,500 |
| LPG | Utilities | ₱3,200 |
| 2 kitchen staff | Labor | ₱26,000 |
| Facebook ads | Marketing | ₱2,000 |
| **Total** | | **₱52,700** |

At **1,500 dishes a month**, each dish must cover:

> ₱52,700 ÷ 1,500 = **₱35.13 overhead per dish**

With a 10% contingency buffer: ₱57,970 ÷ 1,500 = **₱38.65** per dish.

**What this tells you:** the adobo earns ₱186.88 profit per serving
before bills. That's more than the ₱35.13 each plate needs for rent
and bills, so it's healthy. A dish earning only ₱30 per serving would
lose money even though it "has profit."

## 8. Receipt Simulator

Practice an order and see exactly what the receipt would show. **Nothing
is saved, and nothing counts as a sale.**

1. **Build an order:** pick a recipe, set the quantity, tap **Add to
   order**. Repeat for more dishes.
2. **Pricing model:**
   - **VAT inclusive:** your menu price already includes VAT. Most
     Philippine restaurants do this.
   - **VAT exclusive:** VAT is added at the counter.
3. **Service charge %:** if you charge one (for example, 10%).
4. **Customer type:** **Standard** or **Senior / PWD**.
5. **Receipt header:** optionally add your business name, address and
   TIN. These are printed at the top and remembered for next time.

**Example: 2 × Chicken Adobo (₱299) + 1 × Halo-Halo (₱190), VAT
inclusive**

| Receipt line | Standard customer | Senior / PWD |
|---|---|---|
| Subtotal (menu prices) | ₱788.00 | ₱788.00 |
| Less: VAT | — | ₱84.43 |
| Less: SC/PWD discount (20%) | — | ₱140.71 |
| Net VATable sales | ₱703.57 | — |
| VAT-exempt sales | — | ₱562.86 |
| VAT (12%) | ₱84.43 | ₱0.00 |
| **TOTAL DUE** | **₱788.00** | **₱562.86** |
| *With 10% service charge* | *₱858.36* | *₱619.15* |

**Why Senior/PWD pays ₱562.86, not ₱630.40:** the law (RA 9994 and RA
10754) says VAT comes off **first** (₱788 → ₱703.57), and then 20% comes
off that (₱703.57 − ₱140.71 = ₱562.86). Taking 20% off the ₱788 menu
price is a common mistake that undercharges by ₱67.54 on this order.

**Dish margin analysis** (the box inside the receipt) shows what you
really keep:
- **Standard order:** ingredient cost ₱208.56, food cost **29.6%**,
  profit **₱495.01**.
- **Senior/PWD order:** same ingredients, food cost **37.1%**, profit
  **₱354.30**.

Service charge is **not** counted as your profit, because by law (RA
11360) it goes to your staff.

**Printing:** **Print receipt** prints the slip *without* the margin
box, so a customer never sees your costs. Every printout says
"Simulated with Amore Cost — not a valid receipt." It is **not** a BIR
official receipt.

## 9. Margin & POS Simulator

Answers the big question: **at this price, and this many plates a month,
do I make money after all my bills?**

1. **Recipe / dish:** pick one.
2. **Proposed menu price:** starts at the suggested price (before VAT).
   Type a new one or drag the slider. **Reset to suggested** puts it
   back.
3. **Estimated portions sold / month:** how many of *this dish* you
   sell.
4. **Share of OPEX this dish carries:**
   - 100% means this dish alone pays all your bills (the worst case).
   - If it's one of several equal best-sellers, use a smaller share,
     such as 20–40%.

You'll see the food cost %, gross and net margin, **break-even**
(portions per month needed to cover its share of bills), and a **Monthly
profit & loss** chart: Revenue → minus Direct cost → Gross profit →
minus OPEX → Net profit.

**Example: Chicken Adobo, ₱266.96 before VAT (₱299 on the menu), sample
OPEX ₱52,700**

| Scenario | Portions / month | OPEX share | Break-even | Net profit / month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adobo pays **all** the bills | 600 | 100% | 282 portions | **₱59,430** |
| Adobo pays **40%** of bills | 600 | 40% | 113 portions | **₱91,050** |
| Slow month | 300 | 40% | 113 portions | **₱34,985** |

**Reading it:** even if adobo alone paid every bill, you'd break even
at 282 plates a month, about 10 a day. Below break-even, the verdict
tells you how many more portions you need. If the price is at or below
the ingredient cost, it warns you that no sales volume will ever break
even.

## 10. Everyday tasks

**"Chicken just went up at the palengke."**
Ingredient Library → change the chicken price → every linked recipe
updates. Then check the **Below target** filter on Input for dishes that
need a new price.

**"Which of my dishes are underpriced?"**
Dashboard → **Needs your attention**, or Input → **Below target**.

**"Is my food cost healthy?"**
Look at **Avg food cost %** on the Dashboard. 28–32% is the usual
target. Above that, raise the price, shrink the portion, or find a
cheaper supplier (see **Similar ingredients** in the library).

**"I want a nice menu price, not ₱182.47."**
Open the recipe → green Suggested price card → choose **Ends in 9** or
**Round up ₱5**.

**"A senior citizen ordered. What do I charge?"**
Receipt Simulator → add the dishes → **Senior / PWD**. Or see the
Senior/PWD price on each recipe's green card.

**"Should I lower my price to sell more?"**
Margin & POS Simulator → lower the price and raise the portions → watch
whether net profit goes up or down.

**"I'm getting a new phone."**
Old phone: Dashboard → **Download full backup** → send the file to
yourself. New phone: open Amore Cost → Dashboard → **Restore from
backup** → choose the file.

## 11. Words you'll see

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recipe cost** | Everything one serving costs you: ingredients (after yield), packaging, labor, overhead |
| **Direct cost** | Ingredients + packaging + labor only, without overhead. The simulators use this because they subtract your bills separately |
| **Food cost %** | Direct cost ÷ menu price before VAT. Target: 28–32% |
| **Margin %** | Profit ÷ selling price |
| **Markup %** | Profit ÷ cost |
| **Yield %** | The usable share of what you buy after trimming, bones and cooking loss |
| **OPEX** | Monthly fixed bills: rent, utilities, salaries, ads |
| **Overhead per dish** | Monthly OPEX ÷ dishes sold per month |
| **Contingency buffer** | Extra % added to OPEX for surprises |
| **Break-even** | Portions per month where profit exactly covers bills |
| **VAT inclusive / exclusive** | Whether the 12% VAT is already inside the menu price or added on top |
| **SC/PWD discount** | 20% off the VAT-exclusive price for Senior Citizens and PWDs, VAT-exempt (RA 9994 / RA 10754) |

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an account?**
No.

**Is my data safe? Where is it stored?**
Only in this browser, on this device. No one else can see it, including
us. Download a backup regularly.

**Can I use it on my phone and laptop at the same time?**
Not yet. Each device keeps its own copy. You can move data with
**Download full backup → Restore from backup**.

**Does it record my real sales?**
No. Amore Cost decides prices. The Receipt Simulator is practice only.

**Is the receipt a BIR official receipt?**
No. It's marked "not a valid receipt." Use a BIR-registered POS or
official receipts for real sales.

**Can I use it with my POS (Loyverse, KwentaPOS, UTAK, etc.)?**
Yes, side by side. Work out your prices in Amore Cost, then type them
into your POS.

**Does it handle VAT and Philippine taxes?**
It handles VAT (12%), Percentage Tax (3%), custom rates, Senior/PWD
discounts and service charge for **pricing**. For filing taxes, ask
your accountant.

**Is it only for food?**
Yes. It's built for Philippine food businesses: recipes, ingredients
and food cost %.

**I loaded the sample recipes. Will they mix with mine?**
They sit next to yours until you tap **Remove sample recipes**, which
removes only the samples.

**Is it free?**
Yes. Everything in this guide is free.
