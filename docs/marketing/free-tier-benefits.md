# Why Amore Cost is free — the benefits, both sides

Two audiences for this one document: it's the internal rationale for
*why* the free tier is the right call right now, and it doubles as
ready-to-use messaging if a real user (or a piece of content, see
[content-ideas.md](content-ideas.md)) ever asks "what's the catch."
Grounded in [../business/go-to-market.md](../business/go-to-market.md),
[../business/concept.md](../business/concept.md), and
[../business/financial-strategy.md](../business/financial-strategy.md)
— not restated wholesale, just pulled into one place with the "why"
made explicit.

## For the business owner using it

1. **Zero risk to try.** No signup, no card, no trial countdown — price
   a real product today and decide if it's useful before committing to
   anything at all.
2. **Not a crippled trial.** The entire free tier is the real v0
   calculator — cost, price, and break-even, across all three
   verticals (food/service/resale). Nothing about it is a teaser
   version designed to frustrate you into paying.
3. **No lock-in anxiety.** Local-only architecture means the data is
   genuinely yours, sitting in your own browser — not held by a vendor
   you'd have to keep paying to keep access to.
4. **No hidden costs.** There's no billing system at all yet, so
   there's no risk of a surprise charge, a price hike, or a forced
   upgrade later.
5. **Answers "is this worth it" before spending a peso** — exactly the
   question this ICP already asks about every tool, per
   `business/concept.md`'s framing of the audience.

## For the business itself — why this is strategy, not generosity

1. **It's the actual sales mechanism.** Per `go-to-market.md`: "the
   product does the selling." A solo founder with no sales team and no
   ad budget needs the free tier to do the convincing no salesperson
   is available to do.
2. **It removes the specific barrier this ICP has.** This market is
   already skeptical of subscriptions and signups — the same
   zero-friction move that worked for Atlas Freelance's own Phase 1,
   not a generic "freemium is popular" assumption.
3. **It currently costs the business ~₱0 to offer.** Local-only
   architecture means no server cost scales with users — no accounts,
   no backend, nothing metered. Free isn't a subsidy today; it only
   starts costing something once real accounts/backend infrastructure
   exists (see `business/financial-strategy.md` §1).
4. **It's the only way to get the actual validation signal needed.**
   The Phase 1/Phase 2 success criteria (`go-to-market.md`) are
   explicitly about *repeat* free-tier usage — proof the tool replaced
   a habit, not a one-time click. That signal doesn't exist unless
   trying the tool has zero friction.
5. **It compounds through word of mouth in a community-dense market.**
   Per `go-to-market.md`'s "community over ads" — something shareable
   without a "but you'd have to pay first" caveat spreads faster in the
   Facebook groups and communities this ICP actually lives in.
6. **It leaves room to monetize a different axis later, without
   breaking trust.** A future paid tier (real accounts, cross-device
   sync — see `financial-strategy.md` §2–3) can be *added* later
   without ever taking away what people already got for free. Avoiding
   that reversal is deliberate, not incidental.

## The honest caveat

This isn't altruism — it's the only viable go-to-market motion for a
solo, bootstrapped founder with no ad budget and no sales team
(`go-to-market.md`'s explicit framing). It's also a real, reversible
choice under active debate, not a permanent commitment: whether the
free tier stays free forever or becomes the foundation for a
differently-monetized paid layer is still an open fork
(`financial-strategy.md` §2). What *is* settled: whatever is free
today shouldn't be retroactively taken away later — that would break
the exact trust this whole strategy depends on.
