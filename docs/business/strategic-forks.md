# Strategic forks

> **Decided 2026-08-16 — Fork 1 (standalone SaaS).** See
> [decisions/0001-standalone-saas-fork-chosen.md](decisions/0001-standalone-saas-fork-chosen.md).
> The comparison below stays as the historical record of why.

Three genuinely different shapes this business could take. Not
mutually exclusive forever, but the *first move* should pick one.

## Fork 1 — Standalone SaaS, compete on product

The plan already scoped in [../product/scope-v1.md](../product/scope-v1.md):
build the app, position as "the upgrade from the sheet you already
bought," distribute into the same PH Business Network-adjacent
communities. Full control, full upside, slowest to first paying
customer — you're building trust from zero in a space where JR
Quilantang already has 160K members and a 2,000+-buyer track record.

**Risk:** you're not just building a product, you're building an
audience from scratch in a niche someone else already owns.

## Fork 2 — A module inside Atlas Freelance, not a new brand

Atlas Freelance already has the exact ICP (Filipino freelancers/small
service businesses), a working peso-first product, and — per its
`docs/business/go-to-market.md` — is actively looking for Phase 3
"integrations" and content angles. A costing calculator is a natural
adjacent module: a freelancer pricing a project already needs to know
their true cost before they generate the proposal Atlas Freelance
already builds.

**Why this might beat a new brand:** zero new distribution problem —
it ships to Atlas Freelance's existing (even if still small) user base,
reuses its tax/VAT logic, and turns "Atlas Freelance" into a broader
business-OS play instead of "invoicing tool." Costing becomes a feature
that makes the core product stickier, not a separate bet.

**Why it might not:** Atlas Freelance's ICP is *service* freelancers.
The food-costing and reseller audience (arguably the bigger, more
proven demand signal — 2,000+ buyers on one template alone) doesn't
naturally live inside a freelance invoicing tool. Bolting food costing
onto Atlas Freelance risks diluting its "built for freelancers"
positioning for an audience that isn't freelancers at all.

**A middle path:** ship the *service*-costing calculator as an Atlas
Freelance module (clean ICP fit), and keep the *food/retail* costing
tool as the separate "Fork 1" brand (Kwenta or otherwise) aimed at a
genuinely different buyer. This splits the original two reference
templates along the same line their original creator did — for good
reason, the ICPs really are different people.

## Fork 3 — Partner with PH Business Network instead of competing

The most contrarian option, and possibly the fastest path to real
users: JR Quilantang has the exact audience (160K members), the exact
trust (2,000+ buyers already paid for the manual version of this idea),
and zero recurring-revenue SaaS product. He has demand; he's missing
the app. You have the app-building capability; you're missing the
audience.

**Shape this could take:**
- Build the SaaS, pitch it to PH Business Network as a white-label or
  co-branded "official app version" of the existing template — revenue
  share instead of a cold-start distribution problem.
- Or, more simply: reach out as a customer/fan first (buy the templates,
  join the community, understand the audience firsthand — cheap
  diligence before committing to build), and gauge appetite for a
  partnership conversation once you have a working prototype to show,
  not just an idea.

**Risk:** dependency on someone else's brand and goodwill; a
partnership conversation can stall for months. This is a "run in
parallel with Fork 1, don't block on it" option, not a bet-the-company
one.
