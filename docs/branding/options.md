Naming direction, not a final decision — pick one to move forward with,
or use these as raw material for something better. All check for a
matching `.ph`/`.com` domain and a clean social handle before committing;
availability wasn't verified here.

## Design principle

Atlas Freelance's name is English and abstract (a map/navigation
metaphor) but its *positioning* is aggressively peso-first and
Filipino-context-first. This product can go one of two ways:

- **A. Same pattern** — English/neutral name, Filipino-first product —
  keeps a consistent portfolio voice if these ever get cross-sold as a
  suite (Atlas for invoicing, this for costing).
- **B. Filipino word as the name itself** — leans harder into "built by
  and for Filipino business owners," which is a stronger hook in a
  category (PH Business Network, Raket.ph) where the whole culture is
  already Tagalog-first in its marketing copy.

## Shortlist — direction B (Filipino word, direct)

1. **Kwenta** — the strongest option. "Kwenta" is already the word
   Filipino entrepreneurs use to ask *is this worth it* ("May kwenta ba
   yan?" = does the math work out / is it profitable). It's literally
   the word for "calculation/account" *and* colloquially means "does it
   make business sense" — the name states the product's entire value
   prop in one word nobody has to translate. Tagline potential: *"Alamin
   kung may kwenta ang presyo mo."*
2. **Puhunan** — means "capital/investment," the word PH micro-business
   owners already use constantly ("bawi na ba ang puhunan ko?" = have I
   recovered my capital?). Strong emotional resonance with the exact
   break-even/margin anxiety this tool solves, though it points more at
   capital recovery than day-to-day pricing.
3. **Presyo** — direct ("price"). Extremely clear, but likely the
   hardest name to get a clean domain/handle for since it's such a
   generic, obvious word in the category (several Raket.ph sellers
   already use "Presyo"/"Presyo Pro" style names informally).
4. **Tamang Presyo** ("the right price") — descriptive, works well as a
   tagline even if not the standalone brand name.

## Shortlist — direction A (English/neutral, portfolio-consistent)

5. **Marginly** — plays on "margin," reads as a modern SaaS name,
   generic enough to expand beyond PH later if that's ever a goal.
   Less emotionally sticky than the Tagalog options for this specific
   ICP.
6. **CostWise PH** — clear and safe, but generic; several "-Wise"
   fintech names already exist globally (risk of brand confusion, e.g.
   Wise the money-transfer company).
7. **Truecost** — states the core mechanic (your *true* cost, not your
   guess) directly; clean, ownable, but loses the PH-specific warmth
   that's working for Atlas Freelance and for JR Quilantang's brand.

## Recommendation

**Kwenta** (or a variant like *KwentaKo* / *MyKwenta* if the bare word's
domain/trademark isn't available) — it's the one name here that *is*
the pitch, needs zero explanation to the exact audience already buying
the Raket.ph templates, and differentiates cleanly from Atlas
Freelance's more neutral naming without breaking portfolio consistency
(both are still unmistakably "built for Filipino business owners").

## Extended shortlist — more textures to react against

Not vetted as deeply as the top 7, but worth having on the table:

8. **Sulit** — "worth it / good value," one of the strongest
   pre-existing positive brand words in PH e-commerce history
   (Sulit.com.ph was a top-3 marketplace pre-Shopee/Lazada). Risk: heavy
   brand-equity baggage attached to that old marketplace; could read as
   dated or confusingly similar to it rather than as a fresh product.
9. **Tumbok** — slang for "spot on / exactly right," playful and
   memorable ("Tumbok ang presyo mo"). Reads younger/more casual —
   good if you want a TikTok-native brand voice, risky if you want the
   product to feel credible to a 40-something carinderia owner.
10. **Singil** — "to charge/collect payment." Strong verb-based name
    (the product literally helps you decide *singil* — what to charge)
    but carries a slightly transactional/impersonal register on its own.
11. **Kita** — "profit/earnings," the actual outcome the user wants
    ("May kita ba ako?"). Simpler and punchier than Kwenta but more
    generic as a standalone brand word (very common in everyday PH
    business speech, harder to own).
12. **Presisyon** — wordplay on "presyo" (price) + "precision." Clever
    but may read as a stretch/pun rather than an instantly clear name —
    worth testing out loud with a few actual small-business owners
    before trusting it.
13. **Puhunan Balik** ("capital recovered") — more of a tagline than a
    name, but strong as a break-even-focused campaign line regardless of
    which name wins: *"Bawi mo na ba ang puhunan mo?"*

## Round 3 — deeper into Filipino business vocabulary

The first two rounds leaned on *price* words (presyo, tumbok, singil).
This round mines the *money-anxiety* words PH micro-entrepreneurs
already reach for — arguably closer to the actual feeling the product
resolves than the price itself is:

14. **Tubo** — "profit" in the exact everyday sense ("May tubo ka ba
    diyan?" = are you actually making money on that?), and it carries a
    nice second meaning ("tubo" also means "sprout/grow"), so it reads
    as both *profit* and *growth*. Short, punchy, easy to say. Risk:
    "tubo" is also the generic word for interest-on-a-loan in some
    contexts, worth a quick gut-check with real users on which meaning
    lands first.
15. **Bawi** — "recover/get back" (as in recovering your capital —
    "nabawi mo na ba puhunan mo?"). Narrower than Tubo (recovery, not
    ongoing pricing), stronger fit for a break-even-focused campaign or
    feature name than the whole-product brand.
16. **Tantya** — "estimate/guess," as in exactly what the user is doing
    today without this tool ("nagta-tantya lang ako ng presyo" = I'm
    just guessing at the price). Naming the product after the *bad
    habit it replaces* is a distinct, slightly riskier angle — works
    best paired with a tagline that flips it ("Tantya" → "Tantya, pero
    Tama" / "estimate, but right").
17. **Kwentahan** — the more social, verb-form cousin of Kwenta ("let's
    reckon/settle the numbers together"). Softer and friendlier than
    Kwenta on its own, good if the brand voice wants to feel like a
    conversation with a trusted kuya/ate rather than a cold tool —
    slightly less crisp as a one-word brand mark, though.
18. **Diskarte** — "resourcefulness/hustle," one of the most widely used
    words in PH entrepreneurship and hustle-culture content (very
    TikTok-native, matches JR Quilantang's own educational-content
    register). Less literally about pricing, more about the *mindset*
    of a scrappy business owner — strong for marketing copy and
    community-building, weaker as a name that explains what the tool
    literally does.
19. **Batayan** — "basis/reference point," directly lifted from the
    tagline draft below ("presyo na may batayan"). Calm, credible,
    slightly more B2B/professional-sounding than the others — good if
    the brand wants to skew toward being taken seriously by an
    accountant or bookkeeper, not just the owner.
20. **Segurista** — PH business slang for someone who plays it safe and
    double-checks before committing money; carries positive connotation
    (careful, not scared) in entrepreneurship circles. Distinctive and
    ownable, though it's an adjective/persona name rather than a
    calculation word — would need to earn its meaning through
    marketing rather than being self-evident on first read.

## Round 3 — feature-first English names (name the mechanic, not the price)

The single biggest product differentiator is the auto-recalculation —
change one ingredient cost, every linked product updates. These try
naming *that*, not the price itself:

21. **CostChain** — states the mechanic directly (costs chained
    together, one change ripples through). Reads more technical/B2B
    than the Filipino options; likely to appeal more to a systems-minded
    founder evaluating tools than to the actual ICP browsing Facebook.
22. **Repriced** — a verb, implies the product does the re-pricing for
    you automatically. Clean, modern SaaS register; loses all PH-market
    warmth if used bare (would need a strong peso-first visual identity
    to compensate).
23. **MarginIQ** — "smart about your margin." Common SaaS naming
    pattern (X + IQ), instantly legible as software, generically
    corporate — closest in tone to something a US competitor would use.

## Round 3 — "-Ko" hybrid pattern (Grab/Lazada/Gcash-style branding)

A recognizable PH consumer-tech naming convention: [word] + "Ko" ("my
[word]"), already proven at scale by GCash, JuanHand, and similar apps.
Possessive framing fits a tool that's explicitly about *your* numbers:

24. **PresyoKo** ("my price") — clean, ownable, immediately legible,
    fits the naming convention this audience already recognizes from
    other PH consumer apps. Probably the most "looks like a real app
    already downloaded on my phone" option on this whole list.
25. **TuboKo** ("my profit") — same pattern, leads with the outcome
    (profit) rather than the input (price) — arguably the more
    motivating of the two since "am I profiting" is the actual anxiety,
    not "what's my price."
26. **CostKo** — the same pattern in English/Taglish, splits the
    difference between direction A and B; slightly awkward since "cost"
    doesn't carry the same natural Tagalog-possessive rhythm "presyo" or
    "tubo" do.

## Round 4 — "Calc-" family (the one that won)

27. **Calcost** ("calc" + "cost") — **chosen, see
    [decision 0003](../business/decisions/0003-brand-name-calcost.md).**
    Short, two syllables, pronounceable, sounds like real software, and
    states the core mechanic (calculating your *cost*) directly. Only
    prior art found: an obscure Node.js developer library of the same
    name — negligible real-world collision risk. Doesn't have
    RepricedIQ's problem of colliding with an established software
    category. Gap: names the cost-input half of the job, not the
    pricing/margin-output half.
28. **Calcul** — considered and rejected in favor of Calcost. Reads as
    an ambiguous, truncated word (cal-COOL? CAL-cul? "calculate" or
    "calculus" cut off), doesn't specify *what* it calculates, and sits
    in an extremely saturated generic "calculator app" naming space
    (Calculo, CALCU, ClevCalc, and hundreds of others already exist) —
    weaker on every axis than Calcost.

## Round 5 — Amore Cost (the current name)

29. **Amore Cost** — **chosen, see
    [decision 0009](../business/decisions/0009-brand-name-amore-cost.md).**
    Oliver's direct call, replacing Calcost. The logo direction went
    through two iterations the same day: a single-stroke merged
    alpha+percent glyph first (0009), then a two-tone ring with motion
    chevrons and a centered α% glyph, recreated from an externally
    generated reference image
    ([decision 0010](../business/decisions/0010-logo-mark-v2-ring-and-chevrons.md))
    — see `src/components/BrandMark.tsx`.

## Quick comparison, round 3 additions vs. the original recommendation

| Name | States the value prop unprompted? | Feels like an app already on your phone? | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kwenta (existing rec) | Yes — "does this make sense" | Medium | Slightly abstract without a tagline |
| PresyoKo | Partial — states topic, not outcome | High (GCash-style pattern) | Very literal, low distinctiveness |
| TuboKo | Yes — "is this profitable" | High (GCash-style pattern) | "Tubo" double-meaning (interest) needs a gut-check |
| Diskarte | No — states mindset, not mechanic | Medium | Needs marketing to earn the meaning |
| Batayan | Partial — states "has a basis" | Low — sounds more B2B/formal | Least warm/casual of the set |

**Updated read:** if the goal is "state the pitch in the name,"
**Kwenta** still wins. If the goal is "look and feel like an app this
exact ICP already trusts because it resembles GCash/JuanHand," **TuboKo**
is the strongest new challenger — it's the only round-3 addition that
both follows a proven PH consumer-app naming convention *and* names the
outcome (profit) rather than just the topic (price).

## Taglines worth workshopping independent of the final name

- *"Alamin kung may kwenta ang presyo mo."* (Know if your price actually
  makes sense.)
- *"Presyo na may batayan, hindi hula."* (A price with a basis, not a
  guess.)
- *"Hindi na kailangan mag-Excel para malaman kung may tubo ka."* (You
  don't need Excel anymore to know if you're profiting — speaks
  directly to the exact migration-from-sheets narrative.)

## Visual/voice direction (early thinking, not decided)

- Given the audience already trusts JR Quilantang's very plain,
  spreadsheet-native, no-frills brand voice, an overly "startup-slick"
  visual identity could actually read as less trustworthy to this ICP
  than something warmer and more grounded — closer to Atlas Freelance's
  practical tone than to a VC-pitch-deck aesthetic.
- Peso sign (₱) as a recurring visual anchor, same instinct Atlas
  Freelance already validated with its go-to-market positioning.

## Before locking a name

- Check `.ph` and `.com` availability, plus Instagram/TikTok/Facebook
  Page handle availability (this ICP lives on those platforms first).
- Search PH SEC/DTI/IPOPHL trademark records for exact or close matches
  — same due diligence Atlas Freelance's legal docs already went
  through (see `../../../Atlas Freelance/docs/business/Legal/` pattern, if
  applicable, or `docs/business/Legal/` conventions in this workspace).
- Say the name out loud in a sentence a target user would actually say:
  *"Ginagamit ko yung Kwenta para malaman kung magkano dapat ibenta ko
  yung [product]."* — if it reads naturally, it's a good sign.
